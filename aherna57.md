# Campus Marketplace
---

## Project summary
The Campus Marketplace is a buying and selling platform designed specifically for college students. 
Instead of using general marketplaces such as Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist, 
students would have a centralized place to buy, sell, or give away items within their college community. 
The goal is to make transactions between students easier, safer, and more convenient by focusing specifically on the needs of a college campus.

## Problem
The main problem this project solves is that students frequently need to buy or sell items, but existing marketplaces are designed for much larger communities. 
Finding another student who needs a textbook, mini-fridge, desk, bike, gaming console, or other item can be difficult. 
Students also have to sort through listings from people who may live far away or have no connection to their university. 
A campus-focused marketplace would make it easier to find relevant listings from people nearby.

## Features 
- Student account creation and authentication
- User profiles and seller ratings
- Creating listings with photos, descriptions, prices, and categories
- Searching and filtering listings
- Saving/favoriting listings
- In-app messaging between buyers and sellers
- Marking items as sold or available
- Reporting inappropriate or fraudulent listings
- Location-based listings focused around the university
- A “Free” category for students giving items away

## Technology 
The application could be developed using JavaScript/TypeScript, React, and Node.js, with a database such as PostgreSQL for storing users, listings, messages, and transactions. 
Git and GitHub would be used for version control and team collaboration. Potentially a mapping API such as Google Maps or Mapbox for displaying approximate item locations.

## Intended users 
The primary users would be college students, particularly students moving into or out of dorms and apartments. 
Students would benefit from being able to find inexpensive items nearby while also making money from things they no longer need.
