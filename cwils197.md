Pantry - Project Proposal

Pantry is a mobile-first app that turns grocery receipts into a running list of what's actually in your kitchen, then suggests meals based on that list instead of a generic recipe database. You forward a receipt, either as a photo or by email, and the app reads through it and adds the items to your inventory. As you cook, it keeps track of what gets used, and it leans toward suggesting meals that use up things before they go bad.

The problem it's solving is pretty ordinary but genuinely annoying. Most people who cook regularly end up with half-used bags of spinach or random cans they bought for one recipe and never touched again. Recipe apps are built around the assumption that you'll shop for a specific dish, but for most people it works the other way: you buy groceries first and figure out dinner later. There are already apps that generate recipes from a list of ingredients, but they usually require you to type that list in by hand, and almost nobody keeps that up past the first week. The actual hard part isn't the recipes, it's keeping the inventory current without making the user do extra work.

The core features are receipt scanning that turns a photo or forwarded email into structured ingredient data automatically, a live inventory view that updates as receipts come in and meals get logged, meal suggestions that only use what's currently on hand and can be filtered by dietary needs or how much time you have to cook, a soft nudge toward recipes that use ingredients close to going bad, and a simple way to mark a meal as cooked so the inventory adjusts itself instead of needing to be edited by hand.

On the technical side, the plan is to build the frontend in Astro, since it's lightweight and works well for an app that's mostly forms and lists. The backend would run on Cloudflare Workers with Hono, which keeps things cheap and fast to build for a semester-length project, with Cloudflare D1 as the database for storing receipts, inventory, and meal history. The Claude API would handle the two trickiest parts: turning messy receipt text into structured ingredient data, and generating meal ideas that are actually constrained to what the user has, instead of just suggesting whatever sounds good.

The people this is for are anyone who cooks somewhat regularly but doesn't plan meals ahead of time - students, people living on their own for the first time, anyone who shops without much of a list and then improvises. It's a small, everyday annoyance rather than a big one, which is honestly what makes it a good fit for an app people would actually keep using instead of trying once and forgetting about it.
